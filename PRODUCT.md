# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary visitor is a professional who arrives via a shared link — a recruiter, a peer engineer, a potential collaborator, or anyone Sebastián hands the URL to — and gives it a quick, evaluating glance. Their job is simple: decide, in under a minute, whether this is a serious engineer worth taking seriously. They are not a captive audience and rarely arrive with a specific task; the site has to earn credibility fast and make the depth of the work visible without demanding effort.

## Product Purpose

This is the personal portfolio of Sebastián Méndez Villegas, a Mechatronics and Cyber-Physical Systems Engineer. It exists to establish broad professional credibility: to show that he builds real, working integrated systems that connect hardware, software, sensors, and cloud — not concepts or slideware. Success is a visitor leaving convinced he ships real things, and ideally clicking into a project or trying a live app to confirm it for themselves.

## Positioning

Sebastián builds complete, working cyber-physical systems end to end — mechanical design, electronics, control, software, and interface — and the proof is live and runnable, not described. Every screen should reinforce "this person ships real systems," backed by working apps and open code rather than claims.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: Primary is **explore the projects** (drive into the featured work and the Projects page). Secondary, for a visitor not ready to dig in, is **try the live apps** — Torke and Capa8 are running products they can open and use immediately.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "A mechatronics engineer who actually builds and ships working robots and tools."
- Belief ladder: (1) this is a real engineer, not a template; (2) the work spans hardware and software, done for real; (3) the projects are substantial and genuinely functional; (4) the proof is one click away — live apps and open repositories; (5) worth remembering, reaching out to, or trusting with real work.
- Proof on hand: Live working web apps — Torke (parametric gear designer, `torke.sebs.mx`) and Capa8 (network-topology generator, `capa8.sebs.mx`); open-source repositories on GitHub (`github.com/sebas30073007`), including the Remote Hands teleoperated mobile-manipulator; project imagery and, on the Projects page, interactive 3D models.

## Brand Personality

Confident, technical, human. The voice is that of a capable engineer who is sure of the work and doesn't oversell it — direct, precise, and legible, never cold or jargon-walled. Confidence shows through the strong crimson-and-ink identity and heavy display type; the human side keeps copy plain-spoken and the experience welcoming rather than intimidating. It should read as expert and approachable at once.

## Anti-references

Explicitly should NOT feel like: the generic dark-mode dev-template portfolio (gradient hero, interchangeable card grid); corporate/sterile stock-photo agency polish; flashy, gimmicky, over-animated style-over-substance; or a dense academic CV wall of text with no visual life. The site avoids all four by leading with real, runnable work and a committed, personal visual identity.

## Design Principles

Show, don't tell — favor working demos, live apps, real imagery, and 3D over adjectives and claims. Engineering-grade precision — alignment, spacing, and detail should read as rigorous, because sloppiness undercuts the entire pitch. Human warmth alongside competence — plain, confident copy and an inviting feel, never intimidating or cold. Substance over spectacle — motion and effects serve comprehension and proof, never decorate for their own sake. One committed identity — the crimson / ink / off-white system and display-serif-plus-Inter pairing carry a distinct personality that generic templates can't.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: body text at ≥4.5:1 contrast (watch red-on-red and muted grays on tinted surfaces), large text at ≥3:1, full keyboard navigation with visible focus states, and honored `prefers-reduced-motion` for the spinning gear, image carousels, and 3D viewers (already partially in place). Interactive project cards and the modal gallery must be operable and labeled for assistive technology.
